Integrative characterization of the heat shock transcription factors in Acorus: evolution and heat-responsive regulatory patterns

With global climate change driving rising temperatures, the persistence and physiological performance of Acorus are increasingly threatened, highlighting the importance of understanding its molecular responses to heat stress. Heat shock transcription factors (HSFs) constitute a major family of transcriptional regulators involved in plant heat-stress responses and thermotolerance. Here, we identified 39 and 20 HSF genes in Acorus calamus and A. gramineus . Conserved motif and domain analyses revealed subfamily-specific structural features, while comparative synteny suggested that polyploidy and differential gene retention contributed to HSF family expansion in A. calamus . Subgenome-level analysis revealed no obvious difference in HSF gene number between subgenomes, whereas retained homeolog pairs exhibited tissue-dependent expression divergence. Promoter, predicted protein–protein interaction, and GO enrichment analyses linked AgHSFs to stress regulation and heat-response networks. Integrated analysis of time-series expression profiles, differential expression, weighted gene co-expression networks, and qRT-PCR revealed diverse temporal responses among AgHSFs and supported AgHSF07, AgHSF12, and AgHSF20 as strongly heat-responsive candidates for further characterization. AgHSF07 and AgHSF12 showed mainly nuclear YFP signals and strong autoactivation in the yeast system, supporting their potential roles as transcriptional activators. Together, this integrated genomic–transcriptomic framework provides insights into HSF evolution and heat-response regulation in Acorus and highlights AgHSF07 and AgHSF12 as promising candidate regulators for further functional studies of thermotolerance.

Unveiling the adaptive strategies in Blumea Balsamifera roots under salt stress through integrated physiological and multi-omics analysis

Background

Soil salinization is a growing constraint on agricultural productivity, yet the adaptive mechanisms of many medicinal plants—particularly Blumea balsamifera, a species of significant economic and pharmacological value—remain poorly understood. In this study, five treatments—CK (control), LS (low salt), MS (medium salt), HS (high salt), and EHS (extremely high salt)—were established to systematically investigate the comprehensive effects of salt stress on the growth, physiological metabolism, and molecular regulatory networks of B. balsamifera.

Results

The results showed that salt stress significantly inhibited the growth of B. balsamifera, causing leaf chlorosis and wilting. In roots, the activities of antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD, CAT) and the level of malondialdehyde (MDA) initially increased and then decreased, while the accumulation of osmoregulatory substances (PRO, SS, SP) and lignin (LIG) progressively increased. Transcriptomic analysis identified 5,701 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) shared between the HS vs CK and EHS vs CK comparisons, which were significantly enriched in functional terms including transcription factor activity, cell wall organization, plant hormone signal transduction. Metabolomic analysis identified 590 and 647 differentially accumulated metabolites (DAMs) in the HS vs CK and EHS vs CK comparisons, respectively. Under moderate stress, flavonoid synthesis and cutin/suberin biosynthesis pathways were significantly enriched, whereas under extreme stress, the phenylpropanoid pathway, carbohydrate metabolism pathway, and oxylipin/jasmonate-related metabolism pathways were significantly activated. Integrated multi-omics analysis further revealed that cutin, suberin, and wax biosynthesis, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and oxylipin/jasmonate-related metabolism are the core synergistic pathways in the root response of B. balsamifera to salt stress, mediating physical barrier construction, chemical defense and structural reinforcement, and lipid signal transduction, respectively.

Conclusion

Collectively, these findings uncover a multilayered adaptive strategy in B. balsamifera roots under salt stress, namely physical barrier formation, chemical-structural defense, and membrane lipid signaling. These results provide theoretical insights and key molecular targets for breeding salt-tolerant varieties and developing stress-resilient cultivation strategies.

Multidimensional molecular strategies for crop photoperiod adaptation and latitudinal expansion-

The traditional regional distribution of agriculture was historically constrained by crops’ inherent dependence on local environmental cues, particularly photoperiod and temperature. Natural domestication and artificial selection have progressively reduced this dependence on native ecosystems, resulting in a profound restructuring of global agricultural patterns. From the perspective of this global restructuring, this study systematically examines the multidimensional molecular mechanisms underlying photoperiodic adaptive evolution, including population-level point mutations, epigenetic modifications, structural variations, and copy number variations. In parallel, we propose that the latitudinal adaptive expansion of crops represents not merely an ecological niche shift, but a complex evolutionary process driven by coordinated genetic reprogramming across the genome, epigenome, and transcriptome. Consequently, reshaping future agricultural landscapes requires moving beyond the constraints imposed by individual climatic factors and establishing a multidimensional framework that integrates environment, genetic traits, and ecological adaptation. Moreover, rather than focusing solely on the speed of regional expansion, future molecular design should promote the coordinated improvement of broad-spectrum latitudinal adaptability and elite yield-quality traits, thereby securing global food safety and agricultural sustainability.

Comparative physiological and photosynthetic responses of three mangrove species to salinity stress and exogenous melatonin

Extreme salinity is a major environmental constraint affecting plant growth and physiological performance in coastal ecosystems. Although mangroves are naturally adapted to saline environments, their tolerance and physiological responses vary among species, and the effects of exogenous melatonin (MT) under different salinity levels remain insufficiently understood. This study compared the physiological and photosynthetic responses of Aegiceras corniculatum, Kandelia obovata, and Rhizophora stylosa under NaCl-only and corresponding NaCl + MT treatments. Seedlings were subjected for 84 days to seven treatments: control (T1), 100 mM NaCl (T2), 200 mM NaCl (T3), 400 mM NaCl (T4), 100 mM NaCl + 100 μM MT (T5), 200 mM NaCl + 100 μM MT (T6), and 400 mM NaCl + 100 μM MT (T7). Each species × treatment combination comprised one container–reservoir treatment experimental unit containing five seedling subsamples. At 200 mM NaCl, predicted maximum leaf area (K) was 14.6% and 43.0% higher in A. corniculatum and K. obovata, respectively, under the corresponding NaCl + MT treatment than under NaCl alone. Severe salinity (T4) net photosynthetic rate (PN) was 70.18% and 64.17% lower in A. corniculatum and K. obovata, respectively compared with the control. Whereas R. stylosa maintained comparatively higher photosynthetic activity across treatments. Furthermore, reductions were observed in other photosynthesis parameters, with A. corniculatum showing the greatest sensitivity, whereas R. stylosa maintained higher photosynthetic activity under saline conditions. Maximum PSII quantum efficiency (Fv/Fm) remained within a relatively narrow range across treatments, whereas photochemical and non-photochemical responses differed among species. Pigment responses were also species specific, at 200 mM NaCl, total chlorophyll in K. obovata was 58.0% higher under T6 than under the corresponding NaCl-only treatment (T3). These findings indicate that MT-associated responses varied with mangrove species and salinity level, with the clearest changes in specific growth, gas-exchange, photoprotective, and pigment traits under NaCl treatments. Because no MT-only treatment was included, the independent effect of MT under non-saline conditions could not be evaluated; moreover, the shared container–reservoir design limited independent treatment-level replication.

Integrative transcriptomic and functional analyses identify TaARF in the auxin response pathway as a hub gene conferring multi-stage heat tolerance in wheat

Heat stress severely impacts the growth, development, and yield of wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) throughout its lifecycle. Identifying key genes regulating heat tolerance is crucial for breeding climate-resilient wheat cultivars. In this study, Zhenongmai 1 (ZNM1), a heat-tolerant wheat cultivar from Zhejiang Province, exhibited a significantly higher seedling survival rate than the heat-sensitive Yangmai 20 after heat stress. At the maturity stage, ZNM1 maintained superior grain yield components under heat stress, retaining 90.05% of its thousand-grain weight (TGW), while YM20 only retained 84.53%. To elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanism underlying heat tolerance in ZNM1, Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA), Gene Ontology (GO), and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses, were performed to screen heat stress-responsive candidate genes. The results revealed that these candidate genes were predominantly enriched in biological processes including auxin metabolism and NAD(P)H dehydrogenase complex assembly, which are critical for wheat heat tolerance regulation. The expression patterns of 11 core candidate genes were verified by quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR), validating the credibility of the transcriptomic data. Heterologous yeast expression assays further confirmed that TaARF positively regulated plant heat tolerance. Additionally, we systematically analyzed the haplotype variations of TaARF among wheat germplasms from different geographical environments and characterized its tissue-specific expression profiles. This study clarifies the key genes and potential molecular pathways associated with wheat heat tolerance, providing valuable gene resources and theoretical references for the genetic improvement of heat-resistant wheat varieties.

Optimization of protoplast cell division and microcallus formation toward somatic embryogenesis in oil palm

Background

Efficient protoplast regeneration remains a major obstacle to the application of somatic hybridization, genome editing and precision breeding in oil palm (Elaeis guineensis); while early developmental stages, from cell wall regeneration and cell division to microcolony and microcallus formation, could be more important, which could be connected easily with the traditional tissue culture system for succeeding plant regeneration. This study aimed to optimize the protopalst culture conditions for cell division and microcallus formation by identifying the most effective combinations of culture systems, basal media and plant growth regulators.

Methods

Protoplasts isolated from different oil palm tissues (embryogenic callus, suspension cultures, and somatic embryos) were investigated by protoplast yield and viability; protoplasts from the embryogenic callus showed the most satisfactory response and was therefore used for subsequent optimization of culture conditions. The protoplasts were cultured using six culture systems (liquid, droplet, agarose pool, well-plate culture, feeder layer, and nurse culture) in combination with eight basal media (BH3, MS, WPM, Y3, KM, B5, N6, and KPR), and the culture effects were evaluated based on protoplast viability, plating efficiency, cell division, microcolony and microcallus formation. Microcallus emerged from the KPR solid medium were transferred to previously established traditional tissue culture system for further somatic embryogenesis and regeneration.

Results

BH3 liquid medium was particularly effective for early protoplast recovery and rapid cell wall regeneration, whereas KPR solid medium combined with the nurse culture system, provided the most favorable conditions for sustained cell division, plating efficiency, microcolony and microcallus formation. Cell division occurred within 3–7 days, followed by microcolony formation after approximately 7 days and visible microcallus formation after 60 days. Microcallus were transferred for further development, somatic embryos and bud regeneration was observed after 90 days and 120 days, respectively.

Conclusions

The present study focused on the early developmental stages of oil palm protoplast culture, and optimized a stage-specific culture strategy for quick microcallus formation; combined with previous oil palm tissue culture system, further somatic embryogenesis and bud regeneration were established successfully. This work not only promoted the protoplast regeneration of oil palm, but also provides a promising strategy for other tropical palm trees such as coconut and betel nut.

Alkaline salt impairs germination recovery more severely than neutral salt in the euhalophyte Suaeda salsa

Introduction

Soil salinization and alkalization increasingly threaten global ecosystem stability, necessitating a clearer understanding of how dominant halophytes respond to complex salt stresses.

Methods

This study examined the differential effects of neutral and alkaline salt stresses on seed germination and early seedling growth of Suaeda salsa, a dominant halophyte in the Yellow River Delta. A two-factor factorial experiment was conducted using three salt types (NaCl, KCl, NaHCO3) across six concentration gradients (0–500 mmol L-1). A two-stage germination trial, comprising an initial stress phase and a post-stress recovery phase, assessed root and shoot growth, fresh mass accumulation, water potential, and germination ratios.

Results

Salt concentration primarily determined germination traits. At comparable molar concentrations, alkaline salt (NaHCO3) exerted significantly stronger inhibition on growth than the neutral salts (NaCl, KCl). Low-concentration NaCl promoted fresh mass accumulation and sustained shoot elongation through efficient osmotic adjustment, whereas alkaline stress severely impaired this capacity, inducing acute physiological drought. The secondary germination trial revealed an “enforced dormancy” strategy: the effects of neutral salt stress were largely reversible, with markedly enhanced germination upon stress relief, whereas alkaline salt stress reduced recovery potential, fundamentally compromising recovery potential.

Discussion

Neutral and alkaline components must be distinguished when evaluating soil degradation. Future ecological restoration in the Yellow River Delta should prioritize amelioration of high-pH alkaline conditions to ensure natural regeneration of halophyte communities.

However, the ecological functions of the rhizosphere microbiome in salt tolerant plants have not been explored to a large extent. Based on this, this study took the halophyte K. foliatum as the research object, and used metagenomics technology to analyze the differences in microbial composition and salt tolerance functional genes between the rhizosphere and bulk soil of K. foliatum in different salt habitats, providing new theoretical basis for salt alkali management. Diffusible signals from Azospirillum brasilense promote rice growth and induce distinct gene expression changes in plant roots

Plants form associations with diverse beneficial microbes, including arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi, rhizobia, and plant growth-promoting bacteria (PGPB). Research on legume-rhizobia symbiosis (LRS) and AM symbiosis has demonstrated that molecular communication between symbiotic partners is essential for initiating these interactions. Genetic and biochemical studies have further identified both plant and microbial signals, as well as host genetic pathways, that regulate these interactions. However, most studies on plant-PGPB interactions have focused on direct application of PGPB to plant roots, leaving the early signaling mechanisms underlying these interactions poorly understood. In this study, we report that diffusible signals from A. brasilense can promote rice growth without direct contact between bacteria and plant roots, 7 days post-treatment (dpt). Using RNA sequencing, we identified 2,516 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in rice roots in response to diffusible signals from A. brasilense at 1 dpt. We identified differential expression of genes involved in flavonoid synthesis, defense responses, receptor activity, transcription factors, and hormone pathways. Comparative transcriptomic analysis of rice roots with or without contact with A. brasilense revealed both shared and unique gene expression patterns. We observed upregulation of certain defense-response genes (PAL, chitinase, thionin), transcription factors (AP2/ERFs, MYBs, WRKYs, and GRAS), and hormone-related (auxin and ethylene) genes without bacterial contact, but downregulation or no differential expression of these genes upon bacterial contact. This pattern suggests that initial induction followed by subsequent suppression of these genes may be crucial for establishing the interaction with A. brasilense. Our transcriptomic analysis also suggests that flavonoids may be required throughout the interaction, whereas nitrate transporters appear to be involved at later stages and likely require contact between plant roots and bacteria. Collectively, our findings reveal that diffusible signals from A. brasilense can promote rice growth and induce distinct gene expression changes in plant roots. These results provide novel insights into the early phase of rice- A. brasilense interaction and highlight the potential for leveraging plant-PGPB signaling mechanisms to enhance crop growth under adverse environmental conditions.

Whole-genome duplication reshapes gene–miRNA regulatory networks underlying improved salt stress tolerance in Medicago sativa

Abstract

Whole-genome duplication (WGD) is a recurrent feature of plant evolution and crop improvement, yet its direct contribution to abiotic stress tolerance remains difficult to disentangle from hybridization effects. Using full-sibling diploid (2x) and neotetraploid (4x) Medicago sativa genotypes generated through bilateral sexual polyploidization, we investigated how WGD alone reshapes physiological performance and gene–miRNA regulatory networks under salt stress. Neotetraploid plants displayed enhanced tolerance to salinity, maintaining higher chlorophyll content, stomatal conductance, and shoot integrity, alongside reduced visual damage compared with diploid siblings. Transcriptome profiling revealed that ploidy level, rather than salt treatment per se, was the dominant driver of gene expression variation. Indeed, WGD resulted in extensive and largely constitutive transcriptional reprogramming, with more than 2,000 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) distinguishing 4x from 2x plants under both control and saline conditions. By contrast, under salt stress, 705 DEGs specifically differentiated tetraploids from diploids and were enriched in receptor-like kinases, phosphorylation pathways, ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, flavonoid biosynthesis, and WRKY- and MYB-centered regulatory modules. Co-expression network analysis identified salt-associated modules linking signaling and antioxidant pathways with stress-related physiological traits. Small RNA sequencing further showed that WGD reshapes post-transcriptional regulation, with limited salt-induced miRNA changes but the emergence of ploidy-dependent miRNA hubs, including miR2593e and a novel miRNA, targeting signaling and defense-related genes. We hypothesize that WGD establishes a stress-ready regulatory architecture in which coordinated transcriptional and post-transcriptional network rebalancing acts as a buffering system that improves alfalfa physiological performance under salinity, without strong induction of canonical salt-responsive genes.

Abstract

Conserved RGF1 peptide signaling regulates root meristem development through ROS in Arabidopsis and rice

Abstract

The root meristem is essential for stem cell maintenance and root development in plants. In Arabidopsis, Root meristem Growth Factor (RGF) peptides and their receptors regulate root meristem size through reactive oxygen species (ROS)-dependent signalling. RGF1-mediated ROS redistribution post-translationally stabilises the root meristem master regulator PLETHORA2 (PLT2). Although genomic studies suggest that RGF–receptor modules are evolutionarily conserved across land plants, their functional characterisation has remained largely limited to Arabidopsis. Functional analyses using Arabidopsis rgfr receptor mutants further demonstrated that OsRGF1-1 is perceived through conserved RGF receptor machinery. Together, our findings provide the first functional evidence that the RGF1–receptor–ROS signalling module is evolutionarily conserved between dicots and monocots in the regulation of root meristem development.

FAMUS: A Few-Shot Learning Framework for Large-Scale Protein Annotation

Abstract

Motivation

Gene function prediction is pivotal in genomic and metagenomic data analysis. Current annotation tools typically rely on the most similar sequence from the query database and struggle to robustly set meaningful hit thresholds, causing a major challenge for reliable annotation.

Results

Here, we present a contrastive learning framework for functional annotation. FAMUS (Functional Annotation Method Using Supervised contrastive learning) compares query sequences to a full array of profile Hidden Markov Models, encoding each sequence’s complete similarity profile, rather than only its top-scoring hit, into a condensed vector space that minimizes the distance between proteins from the same family. Unannotated sequences are incorporated as negative examples during training, enabling robust detection of proteins that fall outside the reference database without requiring user-defined thresholds. FAMUS outperformed KEGG’s native KofamScan for KEGG Orthology annotation and InterPro’s InterProScan for PANTHER family annotation, especially on realistic, sparsely annotated data. We used this approach to build four protein annotation models spanning the KEGG Orthology, InterPro, OrthoDB, and EggNOG databases. FAMUS is a comprehensive, modular framework that supports both pre-defined and user-specific databases. It can be readily integrated into genomic and metagenomic analysis pipelines for accurate, large-scale functional annotation.

Availability and Implementation

All four models are available as a conda package and via our web server at https://app.famus.bursteinlab.org/, and source code is freely available on GitHub at https://github.com/burstein-lab/famus/ as well as a source code snapshot at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23132542.

Dynamic Graph Prior-Guided Latent Adaptation for Generalizable Single-Cell Perturbation Response Prediction

Abstract

Motivation

Predicting transcriptional responses to drug perturbations in unseen cellular contexts is a central challenge in single-cell perturbation modeling. This challenge creates a need for models that combine flexible state transitions with structured biological prior knowledge. Existing data-driven models often struggle to generalize across unseen cell types, while many prior-based models rely on static biological graphs without explicitly conditioning prior influence on perturbation-specific target support.

Results

We propose DGP-LA, a dynamic graph-prior latent adaptation framework that refines biological priors and injects them as gated latent corrections within a cell-state-conditioned transition model. DGP-LA couples a transition backbone with perturbation-conditioned sparse graph refinement. It propagates gene-aligned target support over the refined graph and selectively injects the resulting topology-aware signal into the hidden representation. On Tahoe and NeurIPS, DGP-LA improves Pearson Delta relative to State by 8.7% and 35.8% for generalization across unseen cell types. In zero-shot drug perturbation response prediction on unseen cell types, it improves Pearson Delta relative to CRISP by 79.1% and 67.6%. The model also improves DE-gene recovery, response discrimination, and Hallmark pathway recovery, supporting adaptive latent integration of biological priors for more generalizable and biologically interpretable perturbation modeling.

Availability

DGP-LA’s source code and reproducibility materials are available at https://github.com/bieyl/DGP-LA and archived on Zenodo at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22155398.

Latent causal diffusions for single-cell perturbation modeling

Perturbation screens hold the potential to systematically map regulatory processes at single-cell resolution, yet modeling and predicting transcriptome-wide responses to perturbations remains a major computational challenge. Existing methods often underperform simple baselines, fail to disentangle measurement noise from biological signal, and provide limited insight into the causal structure governing cellular responses. Here, we present the latent causal diffusion (LCD), a generative model that frames single-cell gene expression as a stationary diffusion process observed under measurement noise. LCD outperforms established approaches in predicting the distributional shifts of unseen perturbation combinations in single-cell RNA-sequencing screens while simultaneously learning a mechanistic dynamical system of gene regulation. To interpret these learned dynamics, we develop an approach we call causal linearization via perturbation responses (CLIPR), which yields an approximation of the direct causal effects between all genes modeled by the diffusion. Genotype-phenotype single-cell transcriptomics for massive parallel assessment of genetic variants

Predicting the pathogenic effect of rare genetic variants is significantly hampered by the limited cohort of diagnosed patients and the difficulty in functionally evaluating the effect of existing and novel variants. To address this issue, we leveraged a protein Multiplexed Assay of Variants Effect (MAVE) to functionally assess the effect of ~ 2200 missense variants of the TP63 gene, some of which cause autosomal dominant developmental disorders. The activity of each variant was measured using an optimized fibroblast-to-keratinocyte conversion protocol, and a subset of mutants was validated through multiple orthogonal biochemical and genomics functional assays to assess consistency and robustness. To systematically expand MAVEs beyond predefined assay, we here introduce SCRAMseq (Single Cell RNAseq Associated with MAVEs by sequencing). This generalizable platform leverages full-length scRNAseq for high-throughput, single-cell genotype-to-phenotype mapping. We validate SCRAMseq across different experimental and genetic contexts to demonstrate its reproducibility and accuracy. SCRAMseq provides a robust and versatile workflow poised to accelerate the discovery and dissection of gene variant effects on any potential disease-driving pathway, thereby significantly broadening MAVEs applicability to virtually any possible disease-driving gene. This work introduces SCRAMseq, a generalizable single-cell platform that pairs full-length transcriptomics with genotyping to test the functional effect of thousands of genetic variants simultaneously, bypassing the need for gene-specific functional assays.
