Abstract

Glycosylation plays a critical role in altering the solubility, stability, and bioactivity of plant specialized metabolites. Focusing on Dendrobium huoshanense, Liu et al. conducted a comprehensive genome-wide identification of the UDP-glycosyltransferase (UGT) gene family (Liu et al., 2026). Samarina et al. presented a comprehensive review summarizing the environmental controls governing anthraquinone and flavonoid accumulation in wild plant species (Samarina et al., 2026). By systematically resolving enzymatic steps, regulatory networks, and system-level dynamics, this Research Topic provides a solid foundation for harnessing medicinal plants as sustainable production platforms for high-value bioactive compounds.

Silicon-mediated alleviation of cadmium stress in Pennisetum glaucum: insights from physiology and root transcriptomics analysis | 硅介导缓解珍珠粟（Pennisetum glaucum）镉胁迫：生理学与根部转录组学分析的启示

Abstract

The problem of cadmium (Cd) pollution in soil has attracted much attention. Although Pennisetum glaucum has developed tolerance mechanisms to heavy metals, the accumulation of excess Cd through the food chain threatens human health. Silicon (Si) has a beneficial role in alleviating environmental stress. However, the underlying physiological and molecular mechanisms for alleviating Cd toxicity in P. glaucum are still largely unknown. Therefore, experiments were conducted to explore the effects of Si on the photosynthetic system, antioxidant defense system, and the expression of in P. glaucum under Cd stress. The results showed that Si amendment increased the net photosynthetic rate and chlorophyll content of Cd-stressed P. glaucum by 19.7% and 13.8%, respectively. Moreover, DHAR and MDHAR activities in roots were significantly increased by 53.6% and 63.6% in the AsA-GSH cycle after Si application, while MDA concentration was significantly decreased by 14.6%. Importantly, exogenous Si application dramatically reduced Cd concentration in roots and shoots of P. glaucum by 52.7% and 21.5%, respectively under Cd stress. RNA-seq analysis showed that 6491 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, among which 4429 DEGs were down-regulated and 2062 DEGs were up-regulated. Root transcriptomic analysis revealed that gene expression differed between the Cd and Cd+Si treatments, particularly for genes related to photosynthesis and glutathione metabolism. Contrarily, the expression of genes involved in Cd uptake and transport, such as ABCG36, ABCB11, Nramp5, and Nramp4 (folds were -1.10, -2.52, -2.66, and -1.55, respectively) were down-regulated by the addition of Si. Overall, these above results demonstrate that Si supplementation offers a viable approach to reduce Cd accumulation and ensure the safe production of forage crops.

Harnessing genetic variation for grain quality from diverse wheat landraces | 利用来自多样化小麦地方品种的遗传变异提升籽粒品质

Abstract

Summary The grain quality traits of protein content, grain weight and size are socially and economically important for wheat (Triticum aestivum) breeding. Modern wheat germplasm has limited genetic variation for vital traits, including grain quality, underscoring the need to investigate ancestral varieties/landraces for beneficial allelic diversity. Here, we develop a reliable and sensitive single‐seed hyperspectral imaging‐based phenotyping platform to quantify grain protein, weight and size in 827 diverse wheat landraces, achieving improved predictive accuracy (R 2 = 0.91) vs previous methods. We identified novel genetic variants (or loci) for grain protein content and its heterogeneity between individual grains in a genotype, and grain weight, and validated them through bi‐parental linkage mapping. Many loci we report are rare and absent from modern breeding germplasm. Beneficial haplotypes at these loci improved mean protein content and homogeneity without compromising grain weight; these haplotypes represent valuable genetic variation for agriculture. We identify a stable locus on chromosome 6A contributing to protein heterogeneity within single grains, suggesting homogeneity in protein content could be a selectable trait for breeding. Our findings demonstrate how integration of advanced phenotyping into breeding strategies can harness untapped genetic diversity for grain quality improvement in wheat, providing an opportunity to enhance grain nutritional value.

Control of organ size by TAS3A | TAS3A 对器官大小的调控

Abstract

Summary Plant organ size depends on the coordination of cell proliferation and expansion, a process regulated by numerous factors, including transcription factors and small RNAs. However, how different small‐RNA pathways are integrated with growth‐regulating transcriptional networks remains only partly understood. Here, we studied how the Arabidopsis TRANS‐ACTING siRNA3A (TAS3A)‐derived trans ‐acting small RNA pathway controls organ size in Arabidopsis thaliana . We found that TAS3A overexpression increased leaf and floral organ size, whereas tas3a mutants showed reduced organ growth. We show that TAS3A acts by modulating AUXIN‐RESPONSE‐FACTOR2 (ARF2) activity and that plants expressing a trans ‐acting siRNA (tasiRNA)‐resistant ARF2 allele showed reduced organ growth. In turn, ARF2 repressed several GROWTH‐REGULATING FACTOR (GRF) genes, including GRF3, which is a direct ARF2 target and is also controlled by microRNA miR396. Furthermore, uncoupling GRF activity from ARF2 repression was sufficient to increase floral organ size. Interestingly, whereas the TAS3A‐ARF2‐GRF module regulates leaf and petal growth predominantly through changes in cell proliferation, gynoecium growth involves a more complex balance between cell proliferation and elongation. Together, our results identify ARF2 as a central molecular node connecting tasiRNA‐ and miRNA‐dependent pathways and establish TAS3A as a key regulator of Arabidopsis aerial organ growth.

Climate drivers of mesophyll conductance: temperature and water deficit alter CO 2 diffusion and leaf anatomy in cotton | 叶肉导度的气候驱动因素：温度和水分亏缺改变棉花中的 CO2 扩散与叶片解剖结构

Abstract

Summary Mesophyll conductance (g m) limits CO 2 delivery to Rubisco, yet its responses to temperature, heat and water deficit are poorly resolved in cotton (Gossypium hirsutum). We characterised photosynthetic and g m temperature responses in five Gossypium species, then examined cultivated G. hirsutum and the Australian native Gossypium bickii under combined elevated temperature and soil water deficit to link physiological responses with mesophyll anatomy. Plants were grown under controlled glasshouse conditions. We quantified g m using simultaneous gas exchange and online stable carbon isotope discrimination (Δ, ‰) and measured leaf anatomy. Across species, photosynthesis and g m exhibited broad thermal optima (33–36°C). In G. hirsutum, water deficit at control temperature reduced g m and induced thicker, more porous mesophyll and thicker cell walls, while assimilation declined only under combined heat and water deficit. In G. bickii, g m and assimilation remained stable under combined stress, and g m was stimulated by elevated temperature without anatomical adjustment. g m emerged as a stress‐sensitive trait in cultivated cotton, whereas G. bickii exhibited stress resilience, with responses incompletely explained by anatomical acclimation. Further research on g m responses to environmental stresses and on the mechanisms underpinning these responses is required to inform model development and identify traits for climate resilient crops.

PhyloZoo: a unified framework for phylogenetic network analysis in Python | PhyloZoo：一个用于 Python 中系统发育网络分析的统一框架

Abstract

Abstract Reticulate evolutionary processes (events in which lineages merge, such as hybridization, recombination, and horizontal gene transfer) are widespread across nature but cannot be represented by phylogenetic trees alone. Phylogenetic networks have therefore become an important modelling tool, yet existing software is typically tied to specific inference paradigms and provides limited support for working with multiple network representations in a unified and programmable environment. PhyloZoo is an open-source Python framework that lowers the barrier to developing practical, easy-to-use software for phylogenetic network analysis. It provides data structures and algorithms covering the main representations used in the field, together with dedicated visualization tools and robust I/O for all major phylogenetic file formats. A particular emphasis lies on semi-directed phylogenetic networks, which explicitly represent root uncertainty and have so far received limited support in existing software. By offering a shared, scalable foundation for developing interoperable tools and a combinatorial layer that supports computational proofs and theoretical exploration, PhyloZoo enables reproducible workflows for applied, methodological, and theoretical studies of reticulate evolution. Availability and implementation: PhyloZoo is implemented in Python and installable from PyPI, with source code, documentation, and examples available at https://github.com/nholtgrefe/phylozoo.

Integrative characterization of the heat shock transcription factors in Acorus: evolution and heat-responsive regulatory patterns

With global climate change driving rising temperatures, the persistence and physiological performance of Acorus are increasingly threatened, highlighting the importance of understanding its molecular responses to heat stress. Heat shock transcription factors (HSFs) constitute a major family of transcriptional regulators involved in plant heat-stress responses and thermotolerance. Here, we identified 39 and 20 HSF genes in Acorus calamus and A. gramineus . Conserved motif and domain analyses revealed subfamily-specific structural features, while comparative synteny suggested that polyploidy and differential gene retention contributed to HSF family expansion in A. calamus . Subgenome-level analysis revealed no obvious difference in HSF gene number between subgenomes, whereas retained homeolog pairs exhibited tissue-dependent expression divergence. Promoter, predicted protein–protein interaction, and GO enrichment analyses linked AgHSFs to stress regulation and heat-response networks. Integrated analysis of time-series expression profiles, differential expression, weighted gene co-expression networks, and qRT-PCR revealed diverse temporal responses among AgHSFs and supported AgHSF07, AgHSF12, and AgHSF20 as strongly heat-responsive candidates for further characterization. AgHSF07 and AgHSF12 showed mainly nuclear YFP signals and strong autoactivation in the yeast system, supporting their potential roles as transcriptional activators. Together, this integrated genomic–transcriptomic framework provides insights into HSF evolution and heat-response regulation in Acorus and highlights AgHSF07 and AgHSF12 as promising candidate regulators for further functional studies of thermotolerance.

Unveiling the adaptive strategies in Blumea Balsamifera roots under salt stress through integrated physiological and multi-omics analysis

Background

Soil salinization is a growing constraint on agricultural productivity, yet the adaptive mechanisms of many medicinal plants—particularly Blumea balsamifera, a species of significant economic and pharmacological value—remain poorly understood. In this study, five treatments—CK (control), LS (low salt), MS (medium salt), HS (high salt), and EHS (extremely high salt)—were established to systematically investigate the comprehensive effects of salt stress on the growth, physiological metabolism, and molecular regulatory networks of B. balsamifera.

Results

The results showed that salt stress significantly inhibited the growth of B. balsamifera, causing leaf chlorosis and wilting. In roots, the activities of antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD, CAT) and the level of malondialdehyde (MDA) initially increased and then decreased, while the accumulation of osmoregulatory substances (PRO, SS, SP) and lignin (LIG) progressively increased. Transcriptomic analysis identified 5,701 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) shared between the HS vs CK and EHS vs CK comparisons, which were significantly enriched in functional terms including transcription factor activity, cell wall organization, plant hormone signal transduction. Metabolomic analysis identified 590 and 647 differentially accumulated metabolites (DAMs) in the HS vs CK and EHS vs CK comparisons, respectively. Under moderate stress, flavonoid synthesis and cutin/suberin biosynthesis pathways were significantly enriched, whereas under extreme stress, the phenylpropanoid pathway, carbohydrate metabolism pathway, and oxylipin/jasmonate-related metabolism pathways were significantly activated. Integrated multi-omics analysis further revealed that cutin, suberin, and wax biosynthesis, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and oxylipin/jasmonate-related metabolism are the core synergistic pathways in the root response of B. balsamifera to salt stress, mediating physical barrier construction, chemical defense and structural reinforcement, and lipid signal transduction, respectively.

Conclusion

Collectively, these findings uncover a multilayered adaptive strategy in B. balsamifera roots under salt stress, namely physical barrier formation, chemical-structural defense, and membrane lipid signaling. These results provide theoretical insights and key molecular targets for breeding salt-tolerant varieties and developing stress-resilient cultivation strategies.
